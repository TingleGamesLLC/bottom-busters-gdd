# Rumor test: the ceiling (2026-11-13)

Throwaway test, not game content. Never use these as examples in the game or in prompts.

**What this tests:** can a good rumor exist under RUMOR_RULES.md at all? Claude wrote all 10 by hand, standing in for the strongest model available.
- **Same rules:** the five tests, the two root types and each mouth's one change.
- **Same town:** Sour Grapes Lane, from the twist test.
  - Toby sells lemonade at Toby's Sour Sips.
  - Mildred sorts the town's mail at Mildred's Mailbox Manor.
  - Gary mends nets at Gary's Gnarly Gear.
  - Bev serves coffee at Bev's Boiling Brew.
  - Ozzie writes plays at Ozzie's Over-the-Top Theater.
- **The player is blank,** as in the current design. In the game, code would pick each chain, culprit and root; here they were picked to cover all five culprits and both root types.

## 1. Wrong person, on purpose: Mildred covers for a friend. 4 people

**You hear it from Toby:** "Morning, neighbor! A perfumed love letter to yourself every day for a month? Mildred can barely keep a straight face handing them over. Hey, nothing says 'admirer' like a Sour Sip, two for a nickel. Tell your friends!"

**Back up the chain:**
- **Toby** (added a detail): You've mailed yourself a love letter signed "a secret admirer" every day for a month, Mildred has to hand them to you with a straight face, and you spray them with perfume first.
- **Gary** (bigger): You've mailed yourself a love letter signed "a secret admirer" every day for a month, and Mildred has to hand them to you with a straight face.
- **Bev** (who was embarrassed): You've been mailing yourself love letters signed "a secret admirer," and Mildred has to hand them to you with a straight face.
- **Mildred** (started it): You've been mailing yourself love letters signed "a secret admirer."

**What really happened:** Ozzie mails himself love letters signed "a secret admirer." When Bev asked who keeps getting the pink envelopes, Mildred named you to protect her friend Ozzie.

**Proof (a note):** an envelope from Mildred's dead-letter tray, typed on the theater's typewriter, which is missing its "e." It reads "a s_cr_t admir_r."

## 2. Made up, out of a grudge: Bev. 4 people

**You hear it from Toby:** "Every puppet show this month, your own hankie and everything! They sit you behind the kids now. Hey, a Sour Sip after a good cry? Two for a nickel. Tell your friends!"

**Back up the chain:**
- **Toby** (added a detail): You've cried at every children's puppet show this month, the theater seats you behind the children now, and you bring your own hankie.
- **Mildred** (what it led to): You've cried at every children's puppet show this month, and now the theater seats you behind the children.
- **Gary** (bigger): You've cried at every children's puppet show this month.
- **Bev** (started it): You cried at the children's puppet show.

**What really happened:** nothing. Bev made it up because on Tuesday you told her, in front of customers, that her coffee was lukewarm.

**Proof (a ticket stub):** there was only one puppet show this month, on Tuesday morning. Its stub book shows eleven tickets sold, all of them children's half-price.

## What writing these showed about the rules

- **All five tests can be met at once, by a strong writer.** The rumors lean on the townspeople's jobs (mail, coffee, plays, nets, lemonade), so the player being blank was workable. Each rumor is about how the player supposedly behaves at those places.
- **Half of the comedy is in the truth and the proof,** not just the rumor. A good root, motive and proof detail matter as much as the morning version.
- **Anyone named in the story can't come later in the chain,** because they'd know it's false. In the game, code has to enforce that.
- **Ozzie's later swap was the one rule I couldn't make work.** Swapping a name only makes a rumor worse in narrow cases, so none of these 10 use it. That rule needs rethinking.
- **Gary's "one step bigger" works** when it's numbers or how often, never impossible. His spoken line can still say "every single" without breaking the claim.
