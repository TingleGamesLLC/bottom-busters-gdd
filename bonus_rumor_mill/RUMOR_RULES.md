# Rumor Mill: the rumor rules (DRAFT, 2026-11-13)

Decisions so far are David's, 2026-11-13. These rules define what a rumor is and how one is built. Every agent that writes or changes a rumor follows them, and a check enforces them before anything reaches the player. No example stories appear here, on purpose.

## 1. What a rumor is

A version of the rumor about you passes only if all five are true:

1. **It stings.** It would change how the town sees you. Someone would whisper it, and you'd want it cleared.
2. **It's believable.** Someone in town could picture it happening. It can be wrong, exaggerated or unfair, but never impossible or nonsense.
3. **It's one claim.** One thing you supposedly did, said in one breath. Never two stories glued together.
4. **It's petty or embarrassing, not a serious crime.** PG-13, nothing violent, nothing sexual.
5. **It's about you.**

## 2. The truth (the root)

You never did it. When you hear the rumor and think "I didn't do that," you're right. The root is one of these:

- **Wrong person.** Someone else really did a small, slightly embarrassing thing, and the culprit put your name on it, on purpose (to cover for a friend or themselves) or by mistake.
- **Made up.** Nothing happened. The culprit invented it, for a reason (a grudge, a debt, covering for themselves, a stunt).
- **From the second game on:** something you really did last game can be a root too (David, 2026-11-13).

The culprit is whoever first made it about you. The hunt is finding them and what really happened.

The root has one exact small detail that proves the real story: that it was someone else, or that it couldn't have happened. Your evidence carries it.

## 3. How a rumor is built: backwards

1. Code sets up the hunt first: the chain of 3 to 5 people (the culprit first, the person who tells you last), the root type, and which part of the story each person changes.
2. The AI writes the morning rumor first: the version you'll hear, broken into parts (what you supposedly did, how much or how often, where, when, and anyone else in the story). It has to pass all five tests in section 1.
3. Walking back up the chain, each person's one change is undone.
4. The top is the culprit's first version. Above that is the root: what really happened.
5. A judge reads every version, top to bottom, and checks it against section 1, and that each step makes it worse for you.

## 4. Each mouth: one change, in their style

Every change keeps it one believable claim about the same thing, and makes it worse for you, never just stranger.

- **The culprit:** their change is making it about you: putting your name on someone else's story, or inventing it. Their style shows in how they say it.
- **Gary (bigger):** one step bigger. Once becomes a habit, a little becomes a lot, a few people become the whole street. Never impossible numbers.
- **Toby (the plug):** adds one detail that fits the claim and makes it worse. His sales pitch is in how he says it, never in the claim.
- **Bev (drama):** adds who was hurt, shocked or embarrassed by it. Believable.
- **Mildred (merge):** adds what it led to: one consequence tied to the same story. Never a second, separate story (David, 2026-11-13).
- **Ozzie (wrong name on purpose):** if he's the culprit, he puts your name on it. If he's later in the chain, he swaps a different name in the story, such as who was affected (David, 2026-11-13).

## 5. What the player should feel

- **The morning:** "I didn't do that." And they're right.
- **During the hunt:** each person's version is a little closer to the root than the last, so they can tell they're climbing the right way.
- **At the replay:** the laugh is the gap between what really happened and what the town believes.
